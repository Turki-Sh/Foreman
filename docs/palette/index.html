Foreman / Toolbelt / Palette

Colours from something real.

Left alone, an agent gives every site a near-black ground, grey neutrals and a blue or violet accent. The palette tool starts from a thing you name instead, an object, a place, a printed page, and builds three palettes that disagree with each other. You pick one.

How it works.

  1. A thing, not a colour. "Name an object, place, or printed thing that has the colour this site should have." In Survey it brings two or three materials it found in your own images and lets you pick.

  2. Ground before hue. What the page is made of moves the result further than the accent does: paper white, a tinted light, a mid-tone, a warm dark.

  3. Three variants that differ on axes. Ground, accent family, where the personality sits, and contrast strategy. One is always light, whatever the material was.

  4. Measured before you see them. Text, muted text, the accent and the label on the accent, against WCAG's 4.5:1 and 3:1. Nothing reaches you unmeasured.

  5. You pick, it locks. Six tokens, the sentence about real things the palette came from, and where the accent is allowed to appear.

The standards behind it.

Every variant is checked against Foreman's colour standards: the values hold in greyscale, one colour dominates and the accent stays rare, and the harmony has a name, complementary, analogous or monochrome. They are standards, so a variant may break one on purpose, and then it says which and why.

Read the standards, including what the golden ratio can and cannot do, or the tool's source.
