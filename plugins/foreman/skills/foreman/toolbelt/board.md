# Board

A reference board for one build: real sites picked for this brief, shown so they can be compared, ending in principles the owner confirms. It runs on its own or at step 3 of a session.

Stamp every message `**FOREMAN · BOARD**`. Mid-session, carry on from the step you were on once the board is done.

## What the board is for

Not inspiration. Most people have no references and will not go looking, and a list of galleries is a way of not choosing. The board does the looking for them and hands back a short set of real pages, each with the one thing worth taking from it. What comes back is not the board. It is their picks, and the principles you read out of the picks.

## 1. Know the brief first

You need four things: what is being built, for whom, the register, and the shape (one viewport, one scrolling page, several pages). Mid-session you already have them. Standalone, take what their message gives you and ask for the rest in one message, never four. "A board for a ceramics studio, editorial" is enough to start.

## 2. Find specific pages, not galleries

`references/reference-library.md` lists where to look by purpose. Use it as a map for your own search, not as the output.

Six to eight entries, in three groups:

- **In the category, at the ceiling.** Four or five live sites doing this kind of page well, in a register near theirs. Search the galleries for the category, then open each candidate and check it still exists and still looks the way the gallery shows.
- **Outside the category.** One or two from somewhere else entirely, chosen for one principle: a museum's identity, a signage system, a journal, a manual, a record sleeve. The section on non-web sources in `reference-library.md` is where the least derivative results come from.
- **Against the grain.** One page in a direction they did not ask for, labelled as such. It is how they find out whether their register word is the right one.

For each entry, one line on what to look at, written as a principle, never as praise. "The work starts a third of the way down, and the hero is one sentence" is a principle. "Clean, modern design" is not.

**Never invent a URL.** If this session cannot browse, say so in one line, send the three or four gallery rows from `reference-library.md` that match the brief, and stop. A board of guessed links is worse than no board.

## 3. A list or a visual board

| | List in the chat | Visual board |
|---|---|---|
| Works in | Every client, a phone included | Sessions that can write files and capture screenshots |
| Judging a design | They have to open each link | At a glance, side by side, which is what a board is for |
| Cost | Seconds | A file and a few minutes |
| Sharing with a partner or client | Paste it | Send the file |

**The list is the default.** It always works, and it is what you send first.

**Build the visual board when they ask for it, or in Stress test, and only when you can put real screenshots in it.** A board of text cards is a list in a costume. If you cannot capture screenshots in this session, say so in one line and send the list.

The visual board is `assets/reference-board.html`, filled in and saved as `board.html` beside the screenshots, which go in a `board/` folder next to it, one desktop capture per entry at 1440 wide, plus a phone capture at 390 for any entry where the phone version is the point. It is a throwaway, like `brand.html`, and never joins the project. Open it for them if you can. The screenshots are for their eyes. They are not shipped, published, or put in the repo.

## 4. What comes back

Ask one question:

> Which two would you keep, what about them, and which one would you not want?

The one they would not want is not a throwaway. It is usually the most specific thing they say all session.

Then read the principles out of their picks, by the procedure in `references/design-direction.md`: named, buildable, checkable sentences about behaviour, never about values. Read them back for a yes. Mid-session they go into `BRAND.md` at step 3, and the URLs stay out of the brief, because a brief that names a site produces a derivative of it. Standalone, the confirmed principles are the output, and you offer the palette or the session as the next step, once.

## Rules that hold here

- References are reading, not templates. If they want a copy, that is their call: say once what it costs, offer the principle version, then do what they pick.
- Never send the whole library. Six to eight entries, chosen.
- No em dashes or en dashes anywhere, in the chat or in the board file.
- Do not explain the board. Send it.
